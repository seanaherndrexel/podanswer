Learning how to get booked as a podcast guest is the fastest way for a small show to borrow an audience that already listens to podcasts. The pitch is the whole game, and most people send the wrong one. Here is how bookers decide, and what to do on the episode so listeners actually follow you home.

## The shortest route to an audience that already listens

Every person hearing you on someone else's show has already solved the hard part of podcast listening. They own an app, they have a commute or a gym slot, and they have a habit. When a host they trust says your name, the request you are making is small: add one more show to a list they already maintain.

That economics is why guesting outperforms almost everything else a small show can do, with search the only channel that reliably beats it. The mechanics are simple and the discipline is not. Find shows whose audience overlaps yours, send a specific episode idea to the person who books, and follow up. Most people fail at the second step, because they send a bio where an idea belongs. Guesting also slots cleanly into the rest of a [podcast promotion plan](/blog/how-to-promote-a-podcast).

## Where you'll get booked as a podcast guest fastest

Most people who ask how to be a guest on a podcast aim straight at the biggest show in the category, which is the one show with a producer, a booking queue and a hundred pitches sitting in it every week. Aim one or two notches above your own show instead. Those hosts have a real audience, they need a guest every week, and nobody else is fighting you for the slot.

A quick way to sort candidates:

- **Too small.** No reviews, fewer than about ten episodes, no consistent schedule. You will record for an hour and reach almost nobody, though a few of these are worth doing early for the practice and the clip.
- **Right size.** Publishing regularly for six months or more, a visible review count, an audience adjacent to yours, and a track record of booking guests who are not famous.
- **Too big.** Named sponsors, a producer credit, guests with books out. Come back in a year with a stronger case.

Relevance beats size on every measure that ends in a follow. Forty of the right listeners will subscribe to you. Four thousand of the wrong ones will not.

### Where podcast guest opportunities come from

Search Apple Podcasts and Spotify for your topic and work through whoever interviews guests. Read the guest lists of shows you already like, then check where else those guests appeared, because a guest who fits one show usually fits five. Watch for hosts asking for guests on LinkedIn or inside niche communities. Then ask every guest on your own show who else you should be talking to, which produces the warmest introductions you will ever get.

Keep the list in a spreadsheet with six columns: show, host name, booking email, what you would pitch, date sent, date followed up. Twenty rows is a working pipeline.

## Do the listening before you write

Listen to at least two episodes of any show you intend to pitch, one of them recent. You are checking the format, the length and what the host's audience already knows. If your topic has come up three times already, your pitch needs a fresh angle or you are asking the host to repeat themselves on air.

Take one note you can use in the pitch. Naming a specific episode and what you took from it proves in a single sentence that you are not running a template, and hosts sort the two apart in about three seconds.

## How to pitch yourself to podcasts without sending a resume

Most pitches get ignored for one reason: they describe the guest. No host has a guest shortage problem. They have an episode problem, every week, forever. So pitch an episode.

A pitch that works has five parts and fits on one screen.

1. **A subject line that is the episode title.** "Episode idea: why most contractors lose money on their best jobs" beats "Podcast guest inquiry."
2. **One sentence proving you listened.** Name the episode and what you took from it.
3. **The idea, in two or three sentences.** What the episode covers, and what a listener walks away able to do.
4. **Three to five talking points as bullets.** Hosts actually use this part, and it shows them the conversation will have a spine.
5. **One line of credibility and your links.** Why you are the person to say this, in a sentence rather than a paragraph.

Then stop writing. No attachments, no media kit in a first email, no offer to discuss synergies.

Send it to a named person. If the show runs a booking form, use the form, because that is the inbox they check. Follow up once after a week and once after two, then drop it. Short follow-ups convert a surprising number of hosts who meant to reply and lost the email.

### A pitch you can copy

> Subject: Episode idea: the pricing mistake that kills small landscaping crews
>
> Hi Dana, I listened to your episode with Marcus on hiring seasonal help and the part about training costs is exactly what I see with clients.
>
> I'd like to propose an episode on how crews price by the hour when they should price by the job, and why it shows up as a cash problem two seasons later.
>
> We could cover: the three signs you're underpriced, how to reprice an existing client without losing them, the one number to track weekly, and what to do when a competitor undercuts you.
>
> I've run a 14-person crew for nine years and now consult for about 30 companies. Show and site linked below. Happy to record any time that suits you.

Specific, useful, and cheap for a busy host to approve.

## What to send once they say yes

Make the host's job easy and you get invited back and recommended sideways to other hosts.

- A short bio in two lengths, one sentence and one paragraph, so they can use whichever fits.
- A headshot and your show artwork.
- The exact URL and spelling of your podcast, plus your handle.
- Three to five questions you answer well, phrased as questions.
- Any link you plan to mention, ready to drop into the show notes.

Show up early, wear headphones, record in a quiet room with something soft in it, and keep a local backup running. Bad audio is the fastest route to being politely not invited back.

## Talking so that listeners go find you

A good appearance and a useful appearance are different products. Listeners follow you when you have given them something before you ask for anything.

Answer the question inside the first ten seconds, then explain. Tell stories with names, numbers and dates in them, because specificity is what survives the hour between listening and remembering. Say your show's name mid-conversation rather than saving it for the outro, and say it in a searchable form: "the Small Crew Podcast, same name in any app" works, a URL nobody will type does not.

Give one call to action, once, at the end. Choose the single outcome you want, usually a search for the show name and a follow. Two asks return neither. If you offer a resource, keep the link short and mention it twice.

## Getting booked as a podcast guest again, and again

The episode goes live and most guests do nothing with it, which is where the value leaks out of the whole exercise.

Share the episode with your own audience and tag the host. It is the cheapest thank-you available and it often earns the repeat invite on its own. Cut a 45 second clip of your best answer and post it with the question as the caption. Add the appearance to your site and your show notes.

Email the host two weeks later with something useful: a listener question you both received, or a referral to another guest. Hosts remember the people who make their week easier, and referrals travel between shows constantly.

Then mine the episode for your own material. The questions a host asked you are questions your own audience has, which makes them episodes. ## Common questions

### How many shows should I pitch to get one booking?

A specific, researched pitch sent cold converts somewhere between one in five and one in ten. Send ten considered pitches rather than a hundred templated ones. Warm introductions convert far higher, which is the argument for asking every host you meet for a referral.

### How do I get booked as a podcast guest with no audience of my own?

Hosts book interesting over popular. Lead with the expertise, the story or the data you hold, and pitch a topic their listeners want. Plenty of hosts book guests with no podcast and no following, because the episode has to be good rather than famous.

### Should I pay a podcast guest booking service?

You do not need one to start, and pitching for yourself teaches you what lands. Twenty researched pitches you wrote will usually beat a bulk campaign, because a host can tell within a line whether a human listened to their show.
