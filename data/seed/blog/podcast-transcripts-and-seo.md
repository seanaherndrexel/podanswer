Transcripts are now cheap, fast and mostly accurate. Every podcaster is told to publish them for SEO. Here's what actually happens when you do.

## What a transcript gives Google

Before the transcript, Google had your title and a paragraph. After, it has every word you said. That's a real improvement, and it's why transcripts occasionally rank for very specific phrases: a product name, a person's name, an unusual term somebody searched.

## What a transcript doesn't give Google

Structure. A transcript is one long block with no headline that matches a search, no clear topic, no direct answer. Google's job is to match a question to the page that best answers it, and a transcript answers everything vaguely and nothing clearly.

That's why transcript pages rarely rank for the questions your episode actually covers. The content is in there. It just isn't shaped like an answer.

## The transcript is raw material, not the product

Think of the transcript as the interview notes and the article as the story. From one 45-minute episode you can usually pull three or four questions the host answered well. Each becomes a page: the question as the title, the answer up front, the host's own words quoted as proof, the episode embedded for anyone who wants the full version.

Those pages rank because they look like what Google is trying to find.

## When to publish the raw transcript too

If you're a member on PodAnswer, the full transcript is published on your episode page alongside the answer articles. It picks up the long-tail phrases, it makes the episode accessible, and it lets the articles link into the source. Just don't expect the transcript page to be the one that brings traffic. The articles do that.

## Accuracy and cleanup

Automated transcripts are around 95% accurate on clear audio. That's good enough for search and for pulling quotes, as long as a person checks any quote before it's published. Names and technical terms are where the errors cluster.

## Cost

Transcription costs a few cents to about forty cents per episode depending on the service, and a lot of hosting platforms now include it. It's not the expensive part. The expensive part is turning it into something worth ranking, which is the part [PodAnswer does](/pricing).
