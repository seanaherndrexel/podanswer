A lot of small business owners start a podcast because it seems like the thing to do. Six months later they have twelve episodes, forty downloads each, and no idea whether it's working. Here's how to make it work, and how to know.

## A business podcast isn't trying to be a big podcast

A true-crime show needs a hundred thousand listeners to matter. A landscaper's podcast needs about forty of the right ones. If a single homeowner in your service area hears you explain how to price a patio and calls you, the episode paid for itself.

So the goal isn't audience size. It's reaching people at the moment they have the question you answer.

## The episodes that generate leads

The ones that answer the questions your customers ask before they buy. What does it cost. How long does it take. What goes wrong. Should I do X or Y. Every one of those is a question someone is typing into Google this week, and every one is an episode.

Record the answer the way you'd explain it across a kitchen table. That's your content plan for a year.

## The promotion problem, and the fix

Here's where most business podcasts die. Nobody searches Spotify for "how much does a patio cost." They search Google. Your episode isn't on Google because it's audio.

The fix is to publish the answer as a written page that credits the episode and links to it. Now the homeowner with the question finds the page, reads the answer, hears you say it, and knows who to call. This is the entire premise of [PodAnswer](/for-podcasters): every episode becomes a set of pages that rank for the questions your customers have.

## What to measure

Forget downloads. Track three things: readers on your answer pages, clicks to listen or to your website, and inquiries that mention the podcast. Ask every new customer how they found you.
