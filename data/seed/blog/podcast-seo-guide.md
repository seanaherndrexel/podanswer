"Podcast SEO" gets used to mean two very different things. One is getting your show to rank inside Apple Podcasts or Spotify search. The other is getting your episodes to show up on Google. The second one is where the listeners are, and it's the one this guide covers.

## What Google actually sees

Google indexes text. Your audio file is a black box to it. What Google can read is your episode title, your show notes, and any transcript you've published as a web page. For most shows that's a title and two paragraphs, which is why podcasts almost never rank for anything except their own name.

## Transcripts help, but not the way people think

Publishing raw transcripts gives Google more words, and that's better than nothing. But a raw transcript is 8,000 words of rambling with no structure, no clear topic, and no title that matches what anyone searches. Google can tell. Transcript pages tend to rank for odd, accidental phrases and not much else.

## The page that ranks is an answer, not a recording

The pages that rank are the ones built around a single question with a clear answer. That means:

- A title that's the question, phrased the way people type it
- A direct answer in the first paragraph
- Headings that break the answer into its parts
- Quotes from the episode as evidence, credited to the speaker
- The episode embedded so the reader can hear the source
- Links to the show on Apple, Spotify and the web

An episode might contain three or four of these. Each one is its own page. That's how a fifty-episode back catalog becomes a hundred and fifty pages of search traffic.

## Authority is built per subject

Google rewards depth. A site with 60 articles about landscaping business ranks for landscaping questions far more easily than a site with 6. That's why the articles for a show should live together, linked to each other, in a hub dedicated to that show and its subject. It's also why the hub is stronger on an established network than on a brand-new domain of your own.

## The parts that are just plumbing

Once the content is right, the technical side is short: a fast page, a sitemap submitted to Google Search Console, structured data that tells Google the page is an article based on a podcast episode, and no duplicate pages competing with each other. Do these once and forget them.

## What you can expect

New pages take a few weeks to get indexed and a few months to settle into their rankings. Long, specific questions rank fastest. Traffic compounds as the hub grows. An article written today is still working next year, which is the difference between SEO and every other promotion channel.
