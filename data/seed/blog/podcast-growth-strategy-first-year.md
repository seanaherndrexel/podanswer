Here's the plan we'd hand to any educational podcaster who asked how to grow from zero over a year without a budget for ads. Nothing here depends on going viral.

## Months 1 to 2: build the base

Publish weekly. Title every episode as the question it answers. Write show notes that actually summarize the answer. Get on Apple, Spotify and YouTube. Pick one call to action and use it every episode.

Numbers to expect: whatever your friends and network bring. Don't read anything into them yet.

## Months 3 to 4: open the search channel

By now you have ten episodes. Each one answers at least one question strangers search for. Get those written up as articles, published somewhere with authority, linked to the episodes. This is the month the compounding starts, even though you won't see it yet.

If you'd rather not build that yourself, [this is what PodAnswer does](/for-podcasters).

## Months 5 to 6: start guesting

Pitch two shows a month that are a little bigger than yours. Offer a specific episode idea, not a bio. Your search pages are now your credibility: send the host a link to an answer article and they see an expert, not a beginner.

Numbers to expect: first search readers showing up in your dashboard. First noticeable download bumps from guest spots.

## Months 7 to 9: mine the back catalog

You have thirty-plus episodes. Go back through them for questions you haven't written up. Old episodes are the cheapest growth you have; the recording is done and the answers are still true.

Numbers to expect: search readers per day climbing steadily. Downloads per episode roughly double what they were in month two.

## Months 10 to 12: sharpen

Look at which questions bring the most readers and clicks. Record more episodes on those subjects. Drop the segments nobody clicks. Start the email list if you haven't.

Numbers to expect: search is now your largest single source of new listeners, and it's growing without extra effort per week.

## What this plan skips on purpose

Paid ads (fine later, not yet). Directory submissions beyond the big three. Daily social posting. Cross-promo swaps with other zero-audience shows. None of these compound.

## The honest part

This works for shows that teach something. If your show is two friends chatting, search won't save it, because nobody's searching for that. If your show answers questions, the plan above is how those answers find their audience.
