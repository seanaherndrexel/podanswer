Triple your review count and your download numbers will barely move. The mechanics of collecting reviews are simple enough to explain in a sentence: ask early in an episode, ask people who are already listening, and tell them exactly which buttons to press. That is most of the tactic, and a month of doing it well might take you from four reviews to fifteen.

Reviews are still worth collecting. They are not a growth engine, and treating them as one costs you the time you could have spent on a channel that compounds.

## What a review is really doing for you

A review works at one specific moment, when a stranger standing on your show page decides whether to press play. At that moment twelve thoughtful reviews are genuinely persuasive, and zero reviews reads as nobody being home. That is real commercial value, and it is the reason to bother at all.

What a review cannot do is bring that stranger to the page. Nothing about it creates awareness. It converts attention you already paid to earn, which makes reviews a close rate improvement on visitors you already have. Your visitor count is a separate problem with a separate solution.

A second use gets less attention. Reviews are free research. Five people independently naming your interviews as the best part of the show is a stronger signal than any survey you could run, and it should change what you commission next.

## Do podcast reviews affect the charts and search results

The folklore gets thick here. Apple has never published the formula behind its charts, and neither has anyone else. Apple has said publicly that chart rankings are driven primarily by new followers and listener growth over a recent window. Ratings and reviews are widely believed to be a smaller input, and some people in the industry think the rating average carries a little weight while written reviews carry almost none. Treat that as an observed belief rather than a documented fact, and hold it loosely.

The direction of causation is what most people get backwards. Shows with thousands of reviews are not large because of the reviews. They carry thousands of reviews because they are large. Adding forty reviews to a show doing 60 downloads an episode does not buy a chart position, and anyone promising otherwise is selling something.

## How to get more podcast reviews from the people already listening

Your current audience is the entire realistic pool, since strangers do not review shows they have never heard. The whole job is converting a small percentage of existing listeners, and the conversion rate stays low even when the execution is good. A show with 300 listeners an episode that collects three reviews from a month of asking is performing normally.

### Ask in the first five minutes, not the last

Putting the ask at the end is the most common error. Most listeners never reach the end, and the ones who do are usually walking into a building or parking a car. Place it after your cold open, once people know what the episode is about, and hold it under fifteen seconds.

### Name the app and the exact steps

"Leave us a review" is too vague to act on, and most people genuinely do not know where the button lives. Something closer to this works: "If you're in Apple Podcasts, tap the show name, scroll past the episode list, and tap Write a Review. It takes about a minute and it helps people decide we're worth trying."

### Give a reason that is about them, not you

"It helps the algorithm" is unconvincing and probably untrue. "It helps someone in your position figure out whether this show is for them" is honest, and it converts better.

### Ask once per episode, not three times

Repeating the ask in the intro, the midroll and the outro trains listeners to skip you. One clean ask per episode, varied in wording, does the job.

### Ask individuals directly

The highest yield move is unglamorous. When someone emails you, replies to your newsletter or says something generous in a DM, ask that person directly. A personal ask converts at a rate a broadcast ask never will, and ten of those beat a hundred generic mentions.

## How to get more podcast reviews on Apple, and what Spotify allows instead

Friction differs by platform, which is part of why review counts stay low everywhere.

| Platform | What listeners can leave | Practical notes |
|---|---|---|
| Apple Podcasts | Star rating and written review | Written reviews are the ones people read. Requires an Apple account and the app. Reviews are separated by country storefront, so a listener in Canada does not see reviews left in the US. |
| Spotify | Star rating only | There is no public written review. Ratings are collected on the show page and shown as an average once enough have come in. |
| Other apps | Varies widely | Several independent apps collect ratings, and some directories host their own review systems. Little of this is visible to the average listener. |

Two consequences follow. Apple is where your written social proof lives, even when most of your listening happens on Spotify. And telling listeners to "rate and review wherever you listen" produces worse results than naming one app and giving one instruction.

### What not to do

Prizes, discounts and shoutouts offered in exchange for reviews are out. Apple's guidelines prohibit incentivized reviews, and past the policy problem, bought enthusiasm reads as fake to the exact people you are trying to persuade. Asking friends and family to post in a burst fails the same test. A pile of five star reviews with no specifics, all posted in the same week, is transparently hollow.

## The trade you are making when you chase reviews

A month of optimizing your review ask might move you from four reviews to fifteen. That is a genuine improvement to your show page, bought with a month of attention that had other uses.

Put the same month into making your back catalogue findable in search and you own something that returns traffic every month afterward. One article answering a question people actually type into Google can send more new listeners in a quarter than every review you will ever collect. That is the comparison worth running, and it is why we file reviews under hygiene rather than strategy. ## Where reviews genuinely pull their weight

Three moments justify real attention to the review count. Pitching a sponsor is one, because a page of specific, credible reviews supports the case that your audience is engaged, which is one of several things [sponsors weigh before they commit](/blog/how-to-get-podcast-sponsors). Pitching yourself as a guest on a bigger show is the second, for the same reason. The third is being new with an otherwise empty show page, since the jump from zero to a handful of reviews is the largest proportional improvement your page will ever get.

Past roughly twenty solid reviews the returns flatten hard. A show with 300 reviews does not convert visitors meaningfully better than a show with 30.

## Common questions

### Do podcast reviews matter enough to build a whole campaign around?

No. A standing fifteen second ask in each episode and the occasional direct request to an engaged listener is the right level of effort. Anything more elaborate, like review swap groups or gated bonus content, costs more than it returns and can breach platform rules.

### Can I get reviews removed if someone leaves an unfair one?

Generally not, unless the review breaks the platform's content policies, in which case you can report it. One or two critical reviews among a set of positive ones actually helps credibility, because a perfect wall of praise looks manufactured.

### Do podcast ratings and reviews carry across if I switch hosts?

Yes, as long as your RSS feed keeps the same show identity and you redirect properly. Reviews attach to the show listing in each app, not to your hosting account. Changing your feed URL without a redirect is what puts them at risk.
